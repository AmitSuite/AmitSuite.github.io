Privacy Policy

How Amit Automation Suite handles your information — what stays on your machine, what is never collected, and exactly what the optional Google Calendar integration does.

  • Effective date: 27 August 2026
  • Last updated: 27 August 2026
  • Applies to: Amit Automation Suite (desktop application) and this website

In short. Amit Automation Suite is a desktop application that runs on your own Windows PC. Your tender records, documents, bank statement data and settings are stored in a database on that machine. There is no product server, no user account and no analytics. The developer does not receive your business data.

The only external service the application talks to on its own behalf is Google Calendar, and only if you switch it on and sign in. That connection is one-way: the suite writes tender dates to a calendar you choose, and never reads your calendar back into your records. Your data is never sold, rented, or used for advertising or profiling.

1. Who we are

Amit Automation Suite ("AAS", "the application", "the suite") is a Windows desktop application developed and maintained by Amit Jain for Aanya Enterprises. In this policy, "we" and "us" refer to the developer, and "you" refers to the person or organisation using the application.

For any question about this policy, or about how the application handles data, write to 123amitjain@gmail.com.

2. Scope of this policy

This policy covers two things:

  • The Amit Automation Suite desktop application installed on your Windows PC.
  • This website, which exists to describe the product and to publish this policy and the Terms of Service.

It does not cover third-party services you reach through your own browser — for example the Central Public Procurement Portal, your bank's website, or Google's own services. Those are governed by their own privacy policies.

3. Information stored on your device

The suite stores its working data in a single database file inside your own Windows user profile, together with the documents it downloads or generates into folders you choose. This information stays on your machine. It is not uploaded to us and there is no synchronisation service behind the product.

4. What we do not collect

To be explicit about the things the application deliberately does not do:

  • No user accounts. There is no sign-up, no login to a service of ours, and no licence server.
  • No analytics or telemetry. The application does not report usage, feature use, crashes, device identifiers or any other measurement back to us.
  • No advertising and no profiling. Your information is never used to build a profile, target advertising, or train any model.
  • No sale or rental of data. We do not sell, rent, license or trade your information to anyone.
  • No background upload. The application does not copy your database, documents or statements anywhere.

If you contact us for support, we receive only what you choose to send us — for example your email address, a description of the problem, or a file you attach. We use it to answer you and for nothing else.

5. Google Calendar integration

The suite offers an optional, opt-in Google Calendar integration so that tender deadlines appear in the calendar you already use. It is switched off until you deliberately enable it, sign in, and choose a target calendar. If you never use it, the application never contacts Google.

5.1 How sign-in works

Sign-in uses Google OAuth 2.0 with your own Google account. Google's own consent screen opens in your browser and shows you exactly what is being requested; the application never sees or handles your Google password.

5.2 The permission requested, and why

Google OAuth scope requested by Amit Automation Suite
ScopeWhy the application needs it
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar The integration creates and removes calendar events for tender dates on the calendar you select, and keeps them accurate as tenders change. A read-only calendar scope cannot write or delete events, so it would make the feature impossible. The same permission is what lets the application list your calendars so you can choose one, and confirm you have write access to it before it starts.

The application requests no other Google permission. It does not request access to Gmail, Drive, Contacts, Tasks, or any other Google service, and it does not use Google Sign-In to log you into the application itself.

5.3 What is read from your Google account

  • Your calendar list — the names, identifiers and access roles of the calendars available to your account, so that you can pick the target calendar from a list.
  • Your account email address — read once so the application can show you which account is currently signed in.
  • The target calendar's name and your access role on it — so the connection test can tell you whether events can actually be written before you rely on it.

The application does not read the contents of your calendar events, and it never copies calendar data into your tender records. The synchronisation is strictly one-way — your records are the source of truth and the calendar mirrors them.

5.4 What is written to your calendar

Only for tenders you have explicitly opted in. For each of those, the application creates an event containing:

  • A title and description built from patterns you configure in the application, which may include tender fields such as the NIT number, the name of work, the department or an EMD amount. You control those patterns, and therefore you control exactly what is sent.
  • The date or start and end time, and the time zone.
  • Optionally a colour, a recurrence rule and reminders, according to your configuration.

Events created by the application are removed from the calendar when the condition that justified them is no longer true — for example once a tender has been submitted or a refund has been received. If you delete one of these events yourself, the application accepts that and does not recreate it.

5.5 The stored sign-in token

After you sign in, Google issues a refresh token so the application does not have to ask you to sign in again. That token is stored encrypted with Windows DPAPI under your own Windows account, inside the local database — deliberately not in Google's default plain-text file store. It can only be decrypted by the same Windows user on the same machine, and it is never transmitted anywhere except back to Google to obtain a fresh access token.

5.6 Turning it off and revoking access

  • Sign out inside the application. This deletes the stored token, and the application immediately stops contacting Google.
  • Revoke access at Google. Visit myaccount.google.com/permissions and remove Amit Automation Suite. Access ends at once.
  • Clear the calendar. Events already written remain in your calendar until you remove them, because they are yours. Deleting the calendar or its events is done in Google Calendar in the ordinary way.

6. Google API Limited Use disclosure

Amit Automation Suite's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Specifically, in relation to data obtained through Google APIs:

  • It is used only to provide the calendar synchronisation feature described in section 5.
  • It is not transferred to any third party, except as necessary to provide that feature, to comply with applicable law, or as part of a merger or acquisition with your consent.
  • It is never used for advertising of any kind, including personalised, re-targeted or interest-based advertising.
  • It is never sold.
  • It is not read by any human, except with your explicit consent for a specific support issue, where necessary for security purposes such as investigating abuse, or to comply with applicable law.
  • It is not used to develop, improve or train generalised artificial intelligence or machine learning models.

Google account data obtained by the application remains on your device. It is not aggregated, not stored on any server operated by us, and is deleted from the application when you sign out.

7. Portal access and browser automation

Several modules work with the Central Public Procurement Portal and similar sites. They do so through your own installed Chrome or Edge browser, which the application attaches to. It does not operate a hidden browser and it does not proxy your traffic through any server of ours.

  • The browser opens only when you explicitly start a run, and the application never closes it for you.
  • The automation never fills or attempts to bypass a CAPTCHA, and never clicks Login, Search or Submit behind one.
  • It never handles one-time passwords or digital signatures.
  • Where portal credentials are stored for autofill, they are held encrypted (see section 9), masked in the interface, and are never written to logs, screenshots or error messages.

Your interaction with those portals is subject to the portal operators' own terms and privacy policies.

8. Documents, statements and OCR

The document parser reads PDFs, images and spreadsheets — including payment receipts and bank statements. All of that processing happens entirely on your own machine.

  • Both optical character recognition engines and their language data ship inside the application. No document, page or image is uploaded to any OCR service, ours or anyone else's.
  • Bank statement data — account identifiers, transaction narrations, amounts and dates — is stored in your local database only.
  • Password-protected statements are opened locally. The password is either supplied by you at the time or read from encrypted storage; it is never displayed, logged or written in plain text.

9. Passwords, tokens and encryption

Secrets held by the application are encrypted using the Windows Data Protection API (DPAPI) in current-user scope. In practice this means a secret can only be decrypted by the same Windows user account on the same machine — copying the database file to another PC does not reveal them.

This applies to portal credentials, bank statement passwords and the Google refresh token. Across the application, the standing rules are that plaintext secrets exist only briefly in memory while they are being used, and that they never appear in log files, screenshots, exception text or generated documents. Error messages returned by external services are stripped of anything resembling a token or key before they are shown.

The administrator password used to unlock configuration screens is stored as a hash, not as a recoverable password.

10. This website

This site is a set of static pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, embeds no advertising or tracking scripts, loads no third-party fonts or content, and has no contact form or database behind it. Nothing you do here is recorded by us.

The site is served by a static hosting provider. Like any web host, that provider may record standard technical request information such as IP address, timestamp and user agent in its own server logs for security and abuse prevention. We do not have access to those logs as an analytics product and do not use them to identify visitors.

The email and telephone links on this site open your own mail client or dialler. Contacting us is entirely your choice.

11. Disclosure to third parties

We do not share your information, because in the ordinary course of using the application we do not hold it. The limited situations in which information leaves your device are:

  • Google — only the calendar event content described in section 5.4, and only for tenders you opted in, and only while the integration is enabled.
  • Procurement portals and banks — only through your own browser, in the course of work you initiate, exactly as if you had typed it yourself.
  • Support correspondence — anything you choose to send us directly.

We may disclose information we actually hold if required to do so by law, by a court, or by a competent authority. We will not do so voluntarily.

12. Retention and deletion

Because your data lives on your machine, you control retention entirely.

  • Records remain until you delete them in the application or delete the database file. Deleting the database removes all tender, financial and configuration data it held.
  • Documents generated or downloaded are ordinary files in your own folders and are deleted like any other file.
  • The Google token is deleted the moment you sign out, or when you revoke access at Google.
  • Support correspondence is retained only as long as needed to resolve your query. Ask us to delete it and we will.

Uninstalling the application does not by itself delete your database or your document folders — that is deliberate, so an uninstall cannot destroy your business records. Remove them yourself if that is what you intend, and keep a backup first.

13. Your choices and controls

  • Access and correction. All of your data is visible and editable inside the application. No request to us is needed.
  • Export. Tender and financial data can be exported to Excel from within the application.
  • Deletion. Delete records in the application, or delete the database file.
  • Withdraw consent for Google Calendar. Sign out in the application, or revoke access in your Google account, at any time.
  • Complain or ask questions. Write to 123amitjain@gmail.com.

If a data protection law applies to you that grants further rights, contact us and we will assist to the extent we are able — noting that in most cases we do not hold your data and the controls above are the fastest route.

14. Security and its limits

The application is built with a specific security posture: local storage, DPAPI-encrypted secrets, credentials excluded from logs and screenshots, administrator-only configuration screens behind a password, and automation that refuses to take irreversible or identity-bearing actions on your behalf.

It is important to be honest about the limits of that:

  • DPAPI protects secrets from being read on another machine or by another Windows user. It does not protect against someone using your own unlocked Windows session.
  • The application does not encrypt the whole database file. If the device itself needs to be protected, use full-disk encryption such as BitLocker.
  • Backups are your responsibility. No system is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.
